Market brief: Friday, 13 November 2026

Equities

  • The Nifty 50 fell 0.61% (155.05 points) to 25,144.30 and the Sensex fell 0.39% to 81,592.45, while the Nifty Midcap 100 (down 0.24%) and Nifty Smallcap 100 (down 0.26%) fell less; decliners (2,315) outnumbered advancers (1,503). [NSE Indices; Google Finance; NSE]
  • The Nifty India Railways PSU index fell 1.98%, the steepest decline among sector indices, followed by cement (down 1.81%), realty (down 1.39%) and auto (down 1.07%). [NSE Indices]
  • Business Standard reported during the session that the Nifty Bank, Auto and Realty indices skidded about 1% as inflation stoked RBI rate hike fears, with experts expecting retail inflation to stay above the RBI's 6 per cent upper tolerance limit for the next few months. [Business Standard]
  • BusinessLine reported before the open that retail inflation had hit 6.09% and Brent crude had risen to $95.23 per barrel, and CNBC-TV18 reported after the close that the Sensex and Nifty snapped a three-day gaining streak as crude oil prices rose and selling pressure hit several sectors. [The Hindu BusinessLine; CNBC-TV18]
  • Provisional data show FPIs as net buyers of 5,736.55 crore and DIIs as net sellers of 2,253.58 crore, and the India VIX fell 6.74% to 15.50, which suggests the decline was orderly and was not driven by foreign selling. [NSE provisional data; NSE Indices]

Bonds

  • The CCIL 10-year G-Sec yield rose 6.8 bps to 7.3069%. [CCIL]
  • BusinessLine reported in the morning that G-Sec yields hardened on retail inflation breaching the 6% mark, with markets expecting the RBI to raise the repo rate by 25 bps to 5.75%. [The Hindu BusinessLine]
  • The Economic Times reported that government bonds came under selling pressure ahead of a major auction of the benchmark 6.94% 2036 paper, and BusinessLine put the auction at Rs 34,000 crore. [The Economic Times; The Hindu BusinessLine]
  • CNBC-TV18 reported before the Indian open that the US 10-year yield surged to 5.13% on Thursday after aggressive speeches by Fed officials, which may have added to the pressure on Indian bonds alongside the domestic inflation print. [CNBC-TV18; The Economic Times]
  • In RBI data for 13 November, the call money rate was 5.61% and the TREPS rate 5.65%, with net liquidity at -1,70,303 crore (negative means the RBI was absorbing surplus funds). [RBI]

Rupee

  • The RBI reference rate for USD/INR was 94.1997, up 0.46% from the previous fixing, so the rupee weakened against the US dollar. [FBIL]
  • CNBC-TV18 reported at the open that the rupee was pressured by rising crude oil prices and higher US Treasury yields, and BusinessLine reported that it fell 8 paise to close at 94.20 against the dollar after trading between 94.05 and 94.28. [CNBC-TV18; The Hindu BusinessLine]
  • Business Standard reported during the session that India's October merchandise trade deficit rose to a one-year high of $39.88 billion, and The Economic Times reported after the close that the rupee snapped its rising streak on a hawkish Fed and higher oil prices. [Business Standard; The Economic Times]

Global backdrop

  • In the 13 November US session (which closes overnight in India), the S&P 500 fell 0.40% to 7,561.67 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.91% to 25,136.27. [FRED; Nasdaq]
  • CNBC-TV18 reported that the Dow fell as oil and yields rebounded, with Nvidia at a 52-week low, and that the US 10-year yield rose to 5.13% after aggressive speeches by Fed officials; it also reported New York Fed President John Williams saying inflation is far too high. [CNBC-TV18; CNBC-TV18]
  • In Asia the Hang Seng fell 1.55% and the Shanghai Composite fell 0.59%, and in Europe the DAX slipped 0.08%. [Google Finance]
  • The Nikkei 225 rose 0.53% and the FTSE 100 rose 0.36%. [FRED; Google Finance]
  • BusinessLine's report that the Nvidia rout wiped out $300 billion of market value suggests technology stocks remained a drag on global risk appetite, consistent with the 0.91% fall in the Nasdaq Composite in the 13 November session. [Nasdaq; The Hindu BusinessLine]

CFO lens

  • The rise in the 10-year G-Sec yield and expectations of another RBI repo increase suggest the cost of new rupee borrowing could rise; Business Standard reported that banks have raised deposit rates after the October repo hike and that lending rates are inching up. [CCIL; Business Standard]
  • Overnight rates near 5.61%, with the RBI absorbing surplus funds in its data for 13 November, suggest short-term funding remains comfortable for now, although ICICI Bank's view that the RBI may raise rates by 25 bps in December points to higher short-term costs ahead. [RBI; The Hindu BusinessLine]
  • USD/INR at 94.1997 and a one-year-high merchandise trade deficit of $39.88 billion suggest continued pressure on the rupee, which may raise rupee costs for importers and improve rupee realisations for exporters. [FBIL; Business Standard]
  • Business Standard reported that India's foreign exchange reserves rose by $2.49 billion to $719.55 billion. [Business Standard]
  • Brent crude at $92.14 per barrel in the latest EIA spot data (11 November) and Business Standard's report of oil trading near $95 on Friday suggest energy and freight input costs may stay elevated. [FRED; Business Standard]
